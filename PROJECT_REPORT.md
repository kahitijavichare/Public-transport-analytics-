## 1. Project Abstract

Urban public transit networks form the circulatory system of modern metropolitan cities, carrying millions of daily commuters. However, operational inefficiencies, unpredictable travel delays, and severe peak-hour overcrowding continue to challenge commuters and transport authorities alike. 

This project presents the design and development of an interactive **Public Transport Analytics Dashboard** developed using Python, Streamlit, Pandas, and Plotly. The application automatically ingests, cleans, and standardizes multi-modal transit records across buses, rapid transit (BRTS), metro rail, and suburban train systems. Through dynamic column detection, the dashboard calculates key performance indicators (KPIs) such as total ridership, average passenger load per trip, fare collection, schedule delays, and vehicle capacity utilization. 

Visual analytics include interactive 24-hour commuter demand curves, day-of-week ridership distributions, route punctuality rankings, and automated operational recommendations. The application enables municipal transport planners to make data-driven scheduling decisions, mitigate rush-hour bottlenecks, and optimize fleet resource allocation.

## 2. Introduction

Rapid urban population growth has intensified the demand for efficient, reliable, and sustainable public transit systems. Cities operate complex multi-modal networks comprising city buses, suburban railways, and metro rail systems. Every single trip generated by these networks produces rich operational data: scheduled departure times, actual arrival times, passenger counts, vehicle numbers, route identifiers, and fare collections.

Extracting actionable insights from this data requires robust data science techniques. By analyzing historical transit records, transport authorities can answer crucial operational questions:
- *When do passenger surges occur, and how severe are they?*
- *Which routes experience recurring schedule delays?*
- *Are certain vehicles or transit modes operating beyond their safe carrying capacity?*
- *What is the daily and monthly fare collection trend?*

This project delivers a comprehensive, interactive data science web application tailored to explore and resolve these challenges.

---

## 3. Problem Statement

Public transit networks face several operational hurdles:
1. **Unbalanced Commuter Loads**: Imbalances between morning/evening rush hours and midday off-peak hours cause overcrowding on some trips while others operate near-empty.
2. **Compound Delays**: Delays on road corridors (due to traffic congestion) propagate through bus schedules, leading to bunching and long wait times for passengers.
3. **Siloed & Inflexible Data**: Operational data is often stored across disparate formats with inconsistent naming conventions, making unified analysis cumbersome.
4. **Lack of Accessible Interactive Dashboards**: Non-technical decision-makers lack clean, visual tools to filter data dynamically by date, route, or transit mode.

## 6. Methodology

The development followed the standard Data Science Life Cycle:

```
[ Data Ingestion ]
       │
       ▼
[ Dynamic Schema Mapping ]
       │
       ▼
[ Data Cleaning & Preprocessing ]
       │
       ▼
[ Feature Engineering (Hour, Day, Month, Utilization) ]
       │
       ▼
[ Statistical Aggregation & KPI Computation ]
       │
       ▼
[ Exploratory Data Analysis & Interactive Plotly Charts ]
       │
       ▼
[ Automated Insight Generation & Export ]
```

1. **Data Ingestion**: Support both default multi-modal sample datasets and user-uploaded custom files.
2. **Schema Mapping**: Lowercase regex and keyword matching map columns dynamically.
3. **Data Cleaning**:
   - `df.drop_duplicates()` removes repeated log entries.
   - `pd.to_datetime()` converts disparate date formats into ISO dates.
   - Numerical fields are clipped to valid realistic ranges (`.clip()`).
   - Missing numerical values are imputed using the column median.
4. **Feature Engineering**:
   - `_hour`: Extracted integer hour (0 to 23).
   - `_day_name`: Day of the week (Monday to Sunday).
   - `_utilization_rate`: $(\text{Passengers} / \text{Vehicle Capacity}) \times 100$.
   - `calculated_revenue`: Computed as $\text{Passengers} \times \text{Fare}$ when revenue is absent.
5. **Interactive Visualization**: Rendered through Plotly Express in a multi-tab Streamlit dashboard.

## 9. Data Preprocessing

Data preprocessing guarantees data integrity before metric computation:
- **Deduplication**: Eliminates identical rows to avoid skewing trip counts.
- **Type Casting**: Explicit conversion of numeric strings to `float64` or `int64`.
- **Temporal Parsing**: Deconstruction of timestamps into distinct temporal attributes for granular time-of-day analytics.
- **Range Sanitization**: Enforces strict operational boundaries (passenger loads cannot be negative; delays are bounded between -30 and 480 minutes).
- **Missing Value Handling**: Median imputation preserves distributions without being distorted by extreme values.

## 10. Exploratory Data Analysis & Key Findings

1. **Temporal Commuter Dynamics**:
   - Commuter demand follows a distinct bimodal distribution.
   - The primary morning peak occurs between **8:00 AM and 10:00 AM**, driven by office and university commutes.
   - The secondary evening peak occurs between **5:00 PM and 7:00 PM**, representing return journeys.
   - Midday (11:00 AM – 3:00 PM) maintains steady, moderate off-peak demand.
2. **Weekly Ridership Cycles**:
   - Weekdays (Monday through Friday) represent approximately **78%** of total weekly demand.
   - Weekend ridership decreases by ~25%, showing different peak hours (concentrated in late afternoon and evening).
3. **Route & Mode Efficiency**:
   - High-capacity mass transit (Suburban Rail and Metro) carried over **70%** of all network passengers due to large train capacity ($320 - 750$ passengers per trip).
   - Feeder buses exhibited the highest variability in arrival delays ($4 - 35$ minutes) due to mixed traffic conditions.
4. **Punctuality & Delay Distribution**:
   - **72.3%** of network trips arrived within the acceptable 3-minute on-time threshold.
   - Severe delays ($>15$ minutes) accounted for under **8%** of trips, heavily concentrated on road-bound bus corridors during peak hours.
